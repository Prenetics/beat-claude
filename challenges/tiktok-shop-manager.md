## The Challenge

You've just taken ownership of IM8's TikTok Shop channel across the **US and UK**. It has real assets and real problems.

**The channel today *(all figures illustrative)*:**
- ~20 SKUs live in Seller Center (singles + bundles); AOV ~$55
- An affiliate base of ~6,000 total creators across Social Snowball and Euka, but only **~15% posting in any given month**
- Monthly TikTok Shop GMV ~$110K, roughly 60% affiliate-organic / 40% ad-supported
- Shop conversion rate ~2.0%; GMV Max running but managed loosely, judged on blended ROI
- **Content output dropped ~53% after a recent agency transition** — rebuilding creator video volume is the single biggest lever

**Your mandate from the Head of Team:** own the channel end to end and get GMV to ~$250K/month within 90 days, without buying the growth purely with discount depth or paid spend. Answer the five parts below — weightings are shown, so **spend your effort where the points are.**

---

## Part 1 — Seller Center & Commission Architecture (25%)

You own the channel in Seller Center. Show the mechanics, not the theory.
- Design the **commission and Shop Ads rate structure** you'd set — open collaboration rate, target-plan (TAP) rates for priority creators, and where you'd deviate by SKU or bundle. Give real numbers and say why.
- Lay out your **campaign calendar and promotion cadence** for the first 90 days (launches, flash mechanics, seasonal moments), and how you protect shop health and claims compliance while running it.
- Name the **two or three creator-incentive mechanics** (challenges, bonuses, tiered payouts) you'd run first, and the exact behaviour each is designed to change.

## Part 2 — Affiliate Activation & Creator Content Pipeline (25%)

The base is big but mostly dormant, and content volume is down 53%.
- How do you take posting affiliates from ~15% to a healthy activation rate? Distinguish **reactivating the dormant 6,000** from **recruiting net-new** — and be honest about which you'd prioritise and why.
- Design the **creator content pipeline**: how you brief for volume *and* quality using narrow, persona-specific angles (not one broad supplement brief), your sample-flow priorities, and how you scale output without sacrificing brand and legal/claims standards.
- What's your line between a creator you give a **direct, managed relationship** versus one you leave in the **open program**? Give the criteria.

## Part 3 — Winner Selection & Paid Amplification / GMV Max (20%)

This is where most operators leak money.
- Describe your **weekly Ads × Affiliate review**: how you select winning creator content for GMV Max, manage the authorized creative pool, and prevent campaign conflicts.
- We currently judge paid on **blended ROI**. Tell us why that's wrong and what you'd judge on instead — and how you'd separate organic from ad-supported orders in reporting so you actually know what's working.
- Give the **rule** you'd use to decide a piece of content graduates from organic into paid, and the rule you'd use to kill it.

## Part 4 — AI / Automation Workflows (15%)

IM8 is an AI-native team. We expect you to use AI daily to work faster and catch more.
- Give **two real examples** of how you use AI in channel/creator work today — the tool, the exact task, and what it saved or caught.
- Describe **one AI workflow you'd build** in your first 30 days here (e.g. creator briefing at scale, content-performance tagging, outreach personalisation) — the tool, what it replaces, and how you'd know it's working.

**Proof gate (scored):** We want proof, not promises. At least **one** of your two examples must include a real artefact — a link, screenshot, or output sample. Redact confidential data if needed, but a redacted artefact still has to be shown. *"I'll show you live in the interview" does not satisfy this.* Part 4 is capped at half marks for any submission whose AI use is entirely described rather than evidenced.

## Part 5 — Judgment Under Pressure (15%)

Pick **TWO** of the following and make a clear call — with the trade-off and a kill-condition.

**(a)** A creator is your single biggest GMV driver this month but keeps making borderline health claims that are a brand and legal risk. Cut them, coach them, or contain them — and how?

**(b)** GMV Max is delivering 4x blended ROAS but a clean read shows most of those orders would have converted organically anyway. Leadership sees the 4x and wants to double spend. What do you do?

**(c)** You can hit the $250K target fast by cranking commission to 30%+ and stacking discounts — but it torches channel margin. Or you can grow slower on content and activation. Which, and where exactly do you hold the line on rate?

**(d)** Your content pipeline is finally scaling, but 70% of the new volume is coming from one persona/angle that's starting to fatigue. Ride it while it works or force diversification now — commit, with your leading indicator for the call.

## What We're Looking For

- Specific, committed numbers — commission rates, activation targets, budget splits — not ranges and hedges
- Real Seller Center / TikTok Shop mechanics (open vs target plans, TAP, GMV Max, Spark authorizations)
- A credible read on the **53% content drop** as the core problem, not a footnote
- Judging paid on **contribution**, not blended ROI, and the reporting to prove it
- Evidence you've actually run a shop — creator relationship calls, incentive design that changed behaviour
- AI woven into the daily work, with a real artefact

## What We're NOT Looking For

- Generic "build a content calendar" or copy-paste TikTok best practices
- A plan that could apply to any brand on any platform
- Buying the whole target with discount depth or paid spend
- Bulk-outreach or agency-oversight thinking dressed up as channel ownership
- AI use that is described but never evidenced (see the Part 4 proof gate)
- Health/supplement claims handled carelessly
